1️⃣                                        Cloud Native Applications

Check then here => https://12factor.net/

Definition:
Cloud-native apps are designed to fully leverage cloud computing environments, including scalability, resilience, and flexibility. They are typically microservices-based, containerized, and dynamically orchestrated.

Key characteristics:

Aspect	Description
Microservices	        App is decomposed into small, independent services that can be developed, deployed, and scaled individually.
Containers	        Each service runs in a container for consistent deployment across environments.
Dynamic Orchestration	Kubernetes, Docker Swarm, or other orchestrators manage scaling, failover, and service discovery.
API-driven	        Services communicate via APIs (REST, gRPC, messaging).
Continuous Delivery	CI/CD pipelines automate build, test, and deployment.
Observability	        Logging, metrics, and tracing enable monitoring and troubleshooting.

Why cloud-native?

Maximizes scalability and resilience.
Simplifies deployment across clouds.
Supports rapid iteration and DevOps practices.

2️⃣ 15-Factor Methodology (Evolution of 12-Factor)

The 15-Factor App methodology is a set of principles for building modern cloud-native applications.
It extends the original 12-Factor App to include newer considerations like observability, security, and microservices patterns.

Here’s a deep dive into each factor:

- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 
I. Codebase

Principle: One codebase tracked in version control per app, many deploys.
Why: Ensures consistency across environments.
Practice: Use Git repositories. Different environments (dev, staging, prod) deploy the same codebase.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

II. Dependencies

Principle: Explicitly declare and isolate dependencies.
Why: Avoid environment drift.
Practice: Use dependency managers (Maven, npm, pip). Containers help isolate runtime.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

III. Config

Principle: Store config in environment variables, not in code.
Why: Code remains portable; config changes per environment.
Practice: SPRING_DATASOURCE_URL=jdbc:mysql://db:3306/mydb.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

IV. Backing Services

Principle: Treat services like databases, message brokers, caches as attached resources.
Why: Decouple app from specific services.
Practice: Reference services via URLs or environment variables.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

V. Build, Release, Run

Principle: Strictly separate build, release, and run stages.
Why: Reduces errors between environments.

Practice:
Build: compile artifacts.
Release: combine build + config.
Run: execute release in the environment.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

VI. Processes

Principle: Execute the app as one or more stateless processes.
Why: Enables scaling and resilience.
Practice: Store user sessions in external storage (Redis) instead of memory.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

VII. Port Binding

Principle: Expose services via port binding instead of relying on runtime injection.
Why: App is self-contained.
Practice: Spring Boot: server.port=8080 and expose via Docker.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

VIII. Concurrency

Principle: Scale out via the process model (horizontal scaling).
Why: Each process handles a workload; scaling is trivial.
Practice: Run multiple instances in Kubernetes Deployment.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

IX. Disposability

Principle: Processes start fast, shut down gracefully.
Why: Supports elasticity, deploys, crash recovery.
Practice: Handle SIGTERM, SIGINT for graceful shutdown.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

X. Dev/Prod Parity

Principle: Keep dev, staging, and production as similar as possible.
Why: Reduces bugs due to environment differences.
Practice: Use containers for local dev to match prod.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

XI. Logs

Principle: Treat logs as event streams.
Why: Aggregators can process logs for monitoring.
Practice: Output logs to stdout/stderr; use ELK or Prometheus.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

XII. Admin Processes

Principle: Run admin/management tasks as one-off processes.
Why: Avoid contaminating long-running app processes.
Practice: DB migrations or maintenance scripts run as separate containers.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

XIII. Observability

Principle: Instrument apps for metrics, tracing, and health checks.
Why: Cloud-native apps are distributed; observability is critical.
Practice: Use Prometheus, Grafana, OpenTelemetry.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

XIV. Security

Principle: Secrets and credentials are handled securely.
Why: Avoid leaking sensitive information.
Practice: Vault, KMS, or environment variables; avoid committing secrets.
- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - 

XV. Microservice Principles

Principle: Each service has a single responsibility and communicates over well-defined APIs.
Why: Supports scalability, independent deployment, resilience.
Practice: REST, gRPC, event-driven architectures.
